# The annulment thread — settled 2026-08-16

*Ruled in full by the Architect before any of it is authored. **Nothing below is written into
`index.html` yet.** This file is the brief. Read it before touching `a2-three`, `a2-choir`,
`a2-annul`, or anything involving Three, Five or Marek.*

---

## Why this exists

The Architect stopped the `a2-law` wave and found the thread does not hold up. The faults,
all confirmed against the file:

1. **The same operation had two opposite outcomes.** Three's annulment: *"the one they took
   out did not survive it."* Five's: *"Neither was thrown away."* Nothing explained the
   difference — so Three's defining wound read as bad luck or bad hands.
2. **The family forcibly annulled a conscious being and nobody blinked.** The whole book is
   about consent — the Choir's crime is that Mirren's *"paperwork was forged to look
   willing"*; `a2-chassis` is a night about asking somebody out loud in a room. Then the
   family straps down a captive and removes it without asking. Not one line addresses it.
   Three least of all, whose want is *no one kept, ever again*, while she sentences Five to
   half a body for life. The file calls it *"by consent"* and nobody was ever asked.
3. **The capture was never staged.** `a2-choir` is a knowledge beat; the next scene opens
   with a Choir convoy commander already restrained in a shack. The hardest thing the family
   ever does happens between beats.
4. **Five had no reason to be there and no role afterwards.** Four lines on the night, two
   much later, and no answer to why an officer submits or why the family takes the risk.
5. **"Taking turns" rhymes with fate one** — the Choir's own mass-market product — and the
   book never noticed.
6. **Three is a human host going by a Choir serial number**, the only one in the cast; every
   other human has a name (Pia, Vic, Hesta, Mirren, Marek Osei) and every numbered character
   is Choir-processed (Nine, Seven, Five).

## The rulings

### 1. Annulment is not a Choir procedure. It is a salvage, and the salvage is what kills.

The Choir has no cure because it has no motive for one. What it does is **recover the
officer-mind for reuse** — and you pull differently when you mean to keep the thing you are
pulling. That is what killed the mind taken out of Three, and why her voice came out split:
the cut was made to preserve the overlay, not the woman underneath.

**Three worked out, in the years since, that if you stop trying to save the overlay, it
lives** — as a passenger, not an officer. The Choir will never do this: an officer who
cannot command is worth nothing to it, and admitting the condition exists is worse than
losing one. **Intent is the variable, not luck and not skill.** This raises Three instead of
cheapening her, and it is why she is the only practitioner alive.

Her existing cold line — *"It was allowed one thing. They all get one thing"* — now reads as
a description of what a passenger is reduced to, not as cruelty.

### 2. The host always comes back intact. The years are simply gone.

Marek Osei went under at one age and woke at another. He is himself; what he lost is nine
years. That is a specific, survivable, terrible grief — **and it is why he cannot stop
talking.** He has nine years of it saved up. (This is already on the page in `a2-annul`'s
opening and now has a reason.)

### 3. Five is asked, and chooses — believing it is about to die.

Five has only ever seen the salvage version, which kills. **Being asked to consent is being
asked to die**, as far as it knows. It says yes anyway. Finding out afterwards that it
survived is its own kind of horror.

This is the beat that repairs fault 2. The family stays the faction that asks. Marek's life
becomes something Five permitted, which is the only thing that makes sharing a body
survivable afterwards.

### 4. Say the fate-one rhyme out loud, and let it hurt.

Fate one is *"the machine herself, reading approved lines"* — **one** entity, broadcast to
millions, that never drives. Taking turns is **two people, both driving, alternately, by
agreement.** One god whispering to many, versus two equals sharing a house.

From outside they rhyme, and a character says so — probably Three, possibly Five. The answer
is that one of them was asked, and that is the *only* difference, and it is the whole book.
State it once, properly. Do not design the resemblance away.

### 5. Three took the number on purpose, and never gives it up.

She refuses her own name because the woman who had it was suppressed for years and she will
not pretend otherwise. **Every time somebody says "Three" she makes them say what was done to
her.** `a2-three` already carries the card *"Ask her what her actual name is"* — that card is
the payoff, and the answer is that she will not give it. Not in Book Two, not in Book Three.

### 6. Five becomes a double agent, and that is why it is in the story.

**Nobody can tell an annulled officer from outside.** It still has the voice, the codes and
the file — and the Choir has no test for the condition because *it does not concede the
condition exists.* The same denial that protects Three (*"an error that was never entered"*)
now protects Five. **The Choir's own secret is the family's way in.**

Five gets the family into the machine vault, which is impossible without it: **the Choir does
not understand the machine, but it knows security.** And Marek wants revenge, so the two of
them want the same thing for different reasons — the only stable version of sharing a body.

### 7. The capture is its own beat, between `a2-choir` and `a2-annul`.

The ambush, what it costs, and the fact that they now hold a Choir officer with a recovery
team already looking. Makes the annulment earned instead of assumed.

### 8. The Choir escalates deniably.

No public hunt — that would concede the process hands people back. The family stops being a
nuisance and becomes something that quietly must not exist. **This is why Book Three goes
underground and after the machine.**

## What must not break

Marek is in the cast of **every beat from `a2-law` to the end** — thirteen of them. "Taking
turns" is not a loose thread, it is wired into the ending:

| beat | what depends on it |
|---|---|
| `a3-dreamers` | *"it is **Five's watch** that misjudges the turn"* — the only place sharing a body has a mechanical cost |
| `a3-stone` | *"Marek Osei is holding my other hand as I say it. Nobody explains to Marek whose hand he is really holding."* |
| `a3-for` | Five speaks; its nature becomes a named part of the plan |
| `a3-seize` | *"The canals reopen under **two pilots who take turns**."* — the book's closing image of the idea |

**So the job is to earn the annulment, not remove it.** Cutting Five costs the ending.
