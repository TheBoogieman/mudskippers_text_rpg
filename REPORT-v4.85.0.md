## What a finished room looks like

Seven cards where there were three. A second version of every one of them. Both endings'
clickable lines wired to the cards they mean. Names on everything. **The writing grew from
3,841 lines to 3,916.**

### The four new ones

**What the pack does on an ordinary day** — Pia. A queue instead of a boss, a wall of
boards nobody owns, and last Tuesday: *"eleven kilos of onions and a letter. The letter was
the job. The onions were because the man with the letter also had onions."*

**What the marsh is like, to her** — Nine's first weather that wasn't a number somebody set
on a Tuesday. You relay it badly, Pia says the marsh is big, and Nine makes you go back and
say the part that mattered: *nobody is in charge of it.*

**Who else knows this address** — Vic: *"Four people. Three of them are in this shack."*
Pia asks who the fourth is. *"Dead. So: us."* Then the kettle goes on and he takes the
excuse.

**What happens to the courier's old life** — the flat goes, the wire goes tonight, the name
stays up on the board a while, which is worse. Said fast by the twelve-year-old, because
she's already paid it.

### Where the room actually pays: the second takes

Ask again and these people go further.

- **Vic's open favour** goes from *"there is one still open"* to the shape of it without
  the name — *"somebody did a thing for me that cost them more than they had"* — and Pia
  says quietly that he has never given her the shape.
- **The shack** stops being about solder. It's a bench he has replaced one piece at a time:
  *"a thing patched right runs longer than a thing that was right to begin with, because
  somebody has been paying attention to it."* Pia says his name. He says he is talking
  about the **bench**.
- **Who else knows this address** stops reaching for the kettle. He walks the causeway
  twice a night, has done for nine years, has never once found anything, and will do it
  again tonight. *"Nine years and one dead friend. Do not make it a poem, Pia."*

## Two of my own mistakes, caught on brand-new prose

Both by the safety net, neither visible by reading:

1. **`Dead. So: three.`** — the game read *three* as **Three**, the woman from Book Two.
   Vic was counting people. Reworded to *"Dead. So: us."*, which is the better line anyway.
2. **Pia said "Hesta's lot send things down"** — and the desk flagged it as summoning
   somebody off-stage. It was right: **Hesta isn't in this room, isn't in this book, and
   the player hasn't met her.** Now "the district".

That's the net doing exactly what it's for, twice, in the first room written to the new
shape.
