# v4.75.0 — "The Short Version"

*Shipped and pushed. What follows is in plain English.*

---

## The problem

Last release I taught the **game** your rule: a mind in a shard can't be heard by anyone
except the person physically holding her. That was the machinery.

**The writing had not caught up.** Twenty-two scenes across the first two books still had
Nine chatting away with people who, by your own rule, cannot hear a word she says. Pia
answering her jokes. Vic answering her question. Seven apologising directly to her. Every
one of those scenes quietly said the chassis doesn't matter — because if she can already
talk to everybody, why would anyone need to build her a body?

## What we did

All twenty-two rewritten to the shape you chose: **she says it → the narration says he
passed it on badly → the room answers the version he gave → she objects.**

Twenty-three new lines went in and **not one of them is a line of dialogue.** That was
deliberate. The courier has no name-plate in this game, and in the AI mode a written
courier line would be taking the storyteller's job. Narration does the carrying, so the
scenes work exactly the same in both modes.

**And the rule turned out to be worth more than it cost.** A few of what it bought:

- Pia says the kindest thing in the book to Nine — *"Said fear is a map"* — and never
  finds out she said it. Nine's reply is now: *"She will never know she said that to me.
  Tell her one day. Not tonight — tonight I would like to have been told something."*
- Nine asks what a gutter is. The courier has to ask the table on her behalf, and Vic
  gives him a very long look. She defends him: *"Tell him it was me. I will not have you
  thought stupid on my account — not over a gutter."*
- Pia shouts *"HEARD that"* down a drainpipe. What she heard was **him** laughing. Nine:
  *"She heard YOU. Nobody has ever heard me. I am taking the credit anyway."*
- Seven, who is in the same condition she is, states the whole arrangement out loud:
  sound reaches her fine, she just can't send. *"That is the one direction that works."*

## The one the automatic checks could never have caught

I played the first chapter through in the game rather than trusting the checks, and found
this in the narration of its closing scene:

> *"…three people who have all just **heard** a stolen mind ration her jokes."*

No dialogue rule was broken. The **narration itself** said all three heard her. It's the
same fault wearing a different coat, and nothing automatic will ever spot it. Fixed, then
I searched both books for that shape specifically — eight other hits, all of them about
people who have bodies, so all fine.

## The safety net

Re-taken by hand, three entries, with a written note saying why each moved. Two were
bookkeeping. The third is the interesting one: the check that tracks *where the shard is*
now fires on a new line in Seven's scene, because the relay only works if the scene has
actually said the courier is holding her — and that scene never had. The safety net
noticing is the writing and the rule agreeing with each other.

**Nothing else moved, across 3,790 lines and nine different checks.** For a pass that
touched twenty-two scenes, that's the result you want: the story got a new texture and
not one fact about the world changed.

## The bar

Boots clean, no encoding damage, all 390 em dashes intact, no broken references, tags
balanced, **all 69 automatic checks pass with none failing**, and I played chapter one
start to finish to read it as a player.

# What's outstanding — and what I need from you

## 1. Book Three breaks in half of all runs

At the end of Book Two the family decides **which of the two minds gets the chassis.**
The other one goes into the housing in the courier's skull, and by your ruling that one is
**silent until Vic fits the speaker rig.**

Book Three was written before that rule existed. **Ten scenes** have one of the two
talking to the room. In the runs where **Seven** got the body, most of those are fine. In
the runs where **Nine** got the body, Seven is talking out loud from inside a skull, and
those ten scenes are wrong.

So: **the biggest branch in your game currently reads correctly down one fork and not the
other.** That's the largest remaining piece of writing in the project.

## 2. The speaker rig scene doesn't exist yet

You ruled it: early Book Three, and **Vic fits it.** It's the last thing he builds, the one
piece of hardware he installs *with* permission, and an apology he can't say out loud.
It's also where the Book Three problem above stops being a problem — everything after it
is audible again.

## 3. Vic's death — the three plants

You asked for three, spread across Books One and Two. His death is already written at the
end (he scanned customers for decades looking for blood the old lock would accept, built
the housing into the courier's skull without asking, used his own living tissue for the
lock, and ran on borrowed time from that night on). What's missing is **the audience
being able to see it coming.** I've drafted three and they're in the questions below.
