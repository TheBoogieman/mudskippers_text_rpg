## You were right about the peril, and now it's pinned

I couldn't confirm it last time, and the reason is worth knowing: **a peril opens a whole
turn above where my test harness was driving from.** It arms when the player *submits* a
line, and I was calling the turn function underneath that. So every attempt came back "it
didn't arm" — not because it doesn't, but because I was standing in the wrong place.

Driven through a real click, it armed on its first eligible turn. And:

> **The night's budget does not run while a peril is live.** It doesn't move when the
> peril opens, and it doesn't move for a turn spent inside it.

This matters more than a detail. Peril is the only real danger in the written game, and a
budget ticking underneath it would punish you twice for one piece of trouble — once by the
thing in the water, and once by the world taking the beat while you dealt with it.

**The check carries its own control**, which is what makes it worth having: it also
measures an *ordinary* turn on the same chapter and requires that one to cost a turn. So a
build where nothing advances at all fails here instead of passing quietly. Measured:
ordinary turn spends 1, arming the peril spends 0, a turn inside it spends 0.

**74 checks — 55 proved, 13 exempt, 6 amber, 0 failing.**
