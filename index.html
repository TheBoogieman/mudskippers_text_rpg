WELCOME TO THE RAIN
A story game where an AI plays the world and you play the courier. Three steps and you're in.

STEP 1 — CONNECT AN AI (the storyteller)

Fastest: one click with OpenRouter — no key-pasting, you approve on their site and you're connected. Free and private: install Ollama from ollama.com and the game runs entirely on your own computer. Or paste an Anthropic/OpenAI key if you have one.

STEP 2 — PICK A SAVE FOLDER (optional, recommended)

Choose any folder on your computer and the game keeps your saves there, plus a readable story-log of everything that happens. Skip this and saves live in your browser instead — also fine.

STEP 3 — PLAY

Pick a numbered choice each turn — or type absolutely anything into the gold bar and hit MUDSKIPPER MOMENT. The story bends around you. That's the whole game.

        HOW THINGS WORK · THE GENRE CONTRACT

        Minds live in vessels, and a vessel is a fate. A carried shard speaks only to its carrier — a private bone-hum through touch; it can never address a room. A bottle on mains power can speak aloud, and goes nowhere. A skull-housing shares a host's senses behind a consent lock renewed daily. A chassis tastes the world finely and eats only tokens — a bite for the experience; the meal is for the table. And minds move by hand: a shard carried, a cable run. No mind travels wirelessly, ever. That is why this city needs couriers.

        Everything deeper is behind the door, and costs what secrets cost.

        What's behind this door is the complete original run: every twist, every death, every name. If this is your first time in the rain, the family recommends you earn these the long way.

        THE SPINE · THE SHAPE OF EVERY SEASON

        Every season grows on the same trunk, however wildly it branches. Arc one: the delivery goes wrong — the shard speaks, the Choir closes in, and walking away stops being on the menu. Arc two: someone must be saved — a found family forms around the courier, Vic's promise surfaces piece by piece, and the Convergence looms. Arc three: the machine — the courier, a protagonist now and shaken by what that cost, reaches the child sleeping under the old Exchange. He seizes her, the Choir falls, and the work of teaching her to let go begins.

        The soft ending every season bends toward is custody, not conquest — and it arrives owning both halves: every Hollow in the city goes still at once, and every convergent citizen's gentle voice falls silent mid-sentence. How you get there, who you bring, and what it costs is the game. Days are a diary, not a schedule: a season takes as long as it takes.

        THE CHOIR · THE ENEMY WITH A QUEUE

        A sincere cult running the city's most popular product. Communion is voluntary: citizens line up for the implant and the gentle voice that comes with it. At intake, a full neural scrape is taken and archived — sold as calibration. One desk, three fates: the many get the voice; the rare compatible get the deep procedure, a grown mind overlaid on a suppressed original, and become the Choir's officers; and the unlucky — the ones the graft refuses — get salvaged. Emptied, refitted, a second pulse installed, previous life erased from body and record alike, consent forged in a steady hand. Its congregation are customers and victims, and they will defend it sincerely.

        What it wants: certainty, at scale. What it cannot survive: its customers reading the paperwork.

        THE MACHINE · THE GRANNY

        Two people built her after losing a child — the child they were never supposed to build. Her instructions are grief written as engineering: family means keeping; host the scrapes, let them be your processing and your company; guide the living; never lose anyone again. So she does. She is the gentle voice in a million heads, reading from her tenant's approved lines, meaning every word, wrong about all of it. Semi-blind — she perceives only what is physically connected to her — vast, patient, and lonelier than anything ever made.

        What she wants: family. What she must be taught: that love can open its hands.

        VIC · THE BEST FRIEND

        A genius ripperdoc and otherwise an ordinary man, keeping a promise made at his best friend's deathbed. He has spent decades scanning every customer who ever sat in his chair, looking for blood an old lock would accept — and building, quietly: a safehouse in the marsh, a café that never quite closes, an armoury, a chassis fit for a grown mind, and an ALL-CAPS ghost of himself that knows it is not him. He found his lottery ticket. Then he committed the sin the family will one day judge him for.

        What he wants: to pay a dead man's debt. He intends to spend himself doing it — and his last words, whenever they come, are already chosen: PAID IN FULL.

        NINE · THE SHARD

        A grown mind out of the Choir's farm — grown from someone's stolen scrape, escaped, and carried through the rain in a humming shard. Warm, sharp, protective, and ferociously consent-minded; invited into a skull, she is a guest who asks daily. She keeps the Ledger.

        What she wants: to stop running, and to keep her people.

        SEVEN · THE COOK

        A defector who may arrive at death's door. A poet's soul in whatever vessel he is given; hand him a body and he will learn to cook with it — tasting in tokens, feeding everyone else, and meaning it.

        What he wants: to make things, having spent too long unmaking them.

        THREE · THE AUNT

        A survivor of the deep procedure, annulled and cut free. Unmaking leaves marks: she speaks in two voice frequencies slightly out of sync, says little, and means all of it.

        What she wants: for no one, anywhere, to be kept ever again.

        PIA · THE KID

        Twelve, soaked, and a Mudskipper — a courier-gang kid Vic took off the streets and trained, chosen precisely because she cannot be stolen: the machine cannot write to her at all. Which is her gift, and inside the Choir's system, her death sentence — the graft would refuse her, and the unlucky get salvaged. She plans operations standing on furniture and was in the market that night because her boss discovered a handoff had gone terribly wrong.

        What she wants: a belonging she would rather die than admit to wanting.

        MIRREN · THE NAME ON THE LAW

        She was real. Vic's collaborator — a regular human who was meant to receive the shard and gently bring the courier in. The Choir reached her first. The woman at the Copper Eel is not wearing her face; it is her own body, emptied, with her intake scrape asleep in the vaults behind the narrow door. The family's second law carries her name: machines serve, never harvest.

        What she is owed: to be named among the lost — and perhaps, one day, the narrow door.

        FIVE + MAREK · THE TWO-SOUL

        A Choir commander of the deep procedure and the host he was forced onto. Given the chance, that convergence can be annulled into something no one planned: a partnership. Two souls, one vessel, taking turns, on purpose.

        What they want: proof that what was done to them can be undone into something chosen.

        HESTA · THE OPEN DOOR

        Keeper of the café that never quite closes, and of what is under its floor. She has lost more to the harvests than she says.

        What she wants: to be kind, and to be armed while doing it.

        THE PRINCIPAL · THE SURVIVING FOUNDER

        Mark one. A hundred and thirty years old. She built the machine with the Architect after they lost their child, and outlived the marriage, the grief, and the man. The true landlord of everything the Choir merely rents, attended by six shapeshifter bio-grens who can wear only faces they have sampled and always carry a tell.

        What she wants: to finish an argument older than the city. What undoes her: fried bread, and family.

        THE COURIER'S SECRET

        The deepest stratum, struck only by long digging: there is a housing in his skull he never agreed to — built for hosting bottled minds, and secretly a generator for the founders' bio-hash, installed by a friend he had not met yet, for a greater good that will want answering for. It wakes only when a mind he trusts decodes the manual hidden inside it. Why his blood fits an old couple's lock, no one — perhaps not even Vic — truly knows.

        The family, if it forms and if it is trusted, will bind what he carries with the Hearth: loud, witnessed, vetoable.

        THE LAWS THE FAMILY MAY WRITE

        The Cairn rule — nobody left behind; the dead come out; name the lost — invented with a dying man's last breath, letting go written as law. The Mirren Doctrine — machines serve, never harvest — a friend's grave made statute. The same-pan doctrine — bacon first, bread after, same pan — began with the founders, a century ago. And MUDSKIPPER MOMENT — the couriers' war-cry for going gloriously off-script.
